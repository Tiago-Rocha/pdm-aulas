You leave the app: what happens to it

Question for the class You are halfway through a long form, switch to WhatsApp and come back an hour later. What may have happened to what you typed?
Answer: if the app was killed to free memory, it starts from scratch and the text is gone, unless the app saved it. That is why good apps save the draft when they go to the background (AppLifecycleState.paused in Flutter). Tie it to something everyone has seen: the game that restarts after a phone call.

Why the system holds apps back

The takeaway: battery and privacy. Doze: with the phone still on the table and the screen off, Android only lets apps run in short windows, further and further apart. On iPhone, the system learns when you open each app and gives it time a little before. Some Android brands (Xiaomi, Samsung, Huawei) cut even more; dontkillmyapp.com has the list.

Four ways to work off screen

Start with the apps: everyone uses at least three of these. The question to choose is always the same: does it have to be at an exact time? Must the person see it? Who knows it is time, the app or a server? The foreground service is the only one that runs nonstop, which is why the system demands the fixed notification. On iPhone there is no free foreground service: only audio, navigation, calls and little else.

Tapping the notification: the right screen

The idea The notification carries a path, and the app hands it to the router. That is a deep link: WhatsApp opens the chat, a parcel app opens the parcel.
With go_router the path is the same as the routes they already have: /ilha/:id. An app can also be opened from a link on a website or in an email (Universal Links on iPhone, App Links on Android), but that needs a verification file on the domain and is outside the project.

In Tempo Açores, and in Flutter

Tomorrow's forecast notification is one of the project extras. flutter_local_notifications does not work in Chrome: whoever picks it must test on an Android phone. Push needs a Firebase account and a server, so it is outside the project. The isolate: the task code starts without the app open, so it cannot see the app state; it reads what it needs from disk, for example from shared_preferences.
