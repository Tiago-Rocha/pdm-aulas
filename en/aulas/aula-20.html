Lesson 20 · Week 9 · Wednesday, 4 November 2026 · 16:00 to 18:00

Written test 1

Written test 1: forty multiple-choice questions on the theory covered up to sprint 1, on paper, closed book, in 60 minutes. After the test, a commented correction of the questions with the most mistakes.

Material: the theory covered up to sprint 1
Arrive ten minutes early. Desks apart, phones switched off and inside the bags, laptops closed. Hand out the test papers face down and only give the order to start when everyone has theirs. Write the start time and the end time on the board.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Show that you have mastered the theory covered so far: Flutter and Dart, screens and layouts, accessibility, state, navigation, network, layers, persistence and authentication.
  • Identify the questions you got wrong and understand why, in the commented correction.
  • Explain the right answer to the questions the class got wrong most.

Test rules

  • Format: on paper, closed book. 40 multiple-choice questions, 4 options, one correct.
  • Duration: 60 minutes. Starts at 16:10, ends at 17:10. There is no allowance for anyone who arrives late: the time counts from the start.
  • Answer: mark the letter on the answer grid in pen. A crossed-out answer counts as wrong; if you make a mistake, ask for the replacement sheet.
  • Wrong answers do not lose marks. Answer every question.
  • It is worth 4 points of the final grade out of 20 (20%): 0.1 points for each right answer.
  • No phone, no smartwatch, no headphones. Any one of these on the desk voids the test.
  • Questions: hand up. The teacher only clarifies the wording, not the material.
  • Leaving: whoever finishes hands in and leaves in silence. Come back at 17:15 for the correction.
Say out loud again that wrong answers do not lose marks, so nobody leaves questions blank. Read the rules out loud before giving the order to start. Anyone who arrives after the first student has handed in can no longer sit the test.

Test material

  • Mobile, Flutter and Dart: the limits of a phone, iOS and Android, the five approaches, Flutter's layers, widgets and build, hot reload, null safety, lifecycle, a single UI thread and 16 ms per frame, Future and async, pubspec and versions.
  • Screens: Material and HIG, gestures, 48 dp targets, the four states, constraints, Row, Column, Expanded, Stack, ListView and GridView, accessibility and tooltip.
  • State and navigation: ephemeral and app state, setState, Provider, Riverpod and BLoC, watch and read, the navigation stack, push and go, deep links, go_router and StatefulShellRoute.
  • Network and layers: HTTP methods and idempotency, status codes, REST and GraphQL, timeouts, JSON and fromJson, repository, notifier, constructor injection, definition of done.
  • Persistence and authentication: where to store what, sandbox, Keychain and Keystore, cache-first and network-first, authentication and authorisation, 401 and 403, JWT, access and refresh tokens, OAuth2 with PKCE, biometrics.

Left out: push notifications, security (OWASP), performance, i18n and lints. There are no questions about code syntax or about the project.

What to bring and what not to bring

Bring

  • Blue or black pen. Two, to be safe.
  • Student card or photo ID.
  • A bottle of water, if you want.

Do not bring to the desk

  • Phone, smartwatch, headphones.
  • Laptop, tablet, notes, books.
  • Pencil: answers in pencil are not accepted.
During the test Do a first pass answering only what you know for sure; go back to the rest with the time that is left. In each question, first eliminate the two options that are clearly wrong. Be suspicious of "always", "never" and "only on iOS".

After the test: commented correction

  1. 17:15 Back in the room. The teacher quickly counts the answers to the questions where the class went wrong most.
  2. Six to eight questions projected, one by one: what the right answer was, why the most chosen option looked right, and which lesson the material is in.
  3. Note down the questions you got wrong. It is the most efficient study list you will have for test 2 and for the project defence.
  4. Results on Moodle by the end of the following week. Appeals in writing, stating the question and the argument, within the following five working days.
While the class goes out for the break, glance over the answer grids and mark the five or six questions with the most mistakes; if there is no time to count, use the ones that usually go wrong: repeating a POST, a 404 that does not throw, read vs watch, token in shared_preferences. Do not reveal individual marks in the room.
