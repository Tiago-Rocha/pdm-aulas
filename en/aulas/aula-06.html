Material 3 vs Human Interface Guidelines

Material 3 (Google)HIG (Apple)
Main navigationNavigation bar at the bottom with 3 to 5 destinations, or a navigation drawerTab bar at the bottom; no drawer
BackSystem button or gesture, from either side; predictive back since Android 14Swipe gesture from the left edge and an arrow in the bar
ButtonsFilled, tonal, outlined, text; FAB for the main actionText and system buttons; no FAB
Dialogs and sheetsCentred dialog; modal bottom sheetCentred alert; action sheet and sheets that slide up from the bottom
TypographyRoboto; a scale with 15 styles (display, headline, title, body, label)SF Pro; Dynamic Type scale controlled by the user
Ask someone with an iPhone and someone with an Android to show the same WhatsApp screen side by side. The differences in navigation and dialogs are obvious at a glance. Give it 5 minutes.

When to follow the platform

  • WhatsApp and Gmail follow each platform: tabs at the top on Android, at the bottom on iOS. Instagram and Spotify have their own design, the same everywhere. Both choices are legitimate.
  • Flutter is Material by default and that is how we are going to work. There are Cupertino widgets for anyone who wants the iOS look, but maintaining both doubles the work.
  • What the user notices, and what you must always respect: the iOS back gesture, each platform's scroll physics, the system permission dialogs, the Android back key. Flutter handles this by default.
  • What the user does not notice: whether the button is "filled" or "bordered", whether the font is Roboto or Inter.

For the tests: Material 3 is Google's design system and Flutter's default; the HIG are Apple's rules. An app can have its own design, but it must respect the system's gestures and conventions.

Gestures users expect

GestureWhat the user expects to happenIf you ignore it
TapImmediate action with visual feedback (ripple, colour change)Taps twice, fires two actions
Long pressContext menu, selection, dragCannot find the "delete" option and goes to the settings
Horizontal swipeGo back (left edge on iOS), change tab, delete a list itemGets stuck on a screen; leaves the app
Pull-to-refreshRefresh the list, with an indicatorReopens the app to see new data
Pinch and double tapZoom on images and mapsCannot read what is in the image

The rule is not to invent. A gesture that only your app uses is a gesture nobody discovers.

Touch, thumb and legibility

  • 48 × 48 dp is the minimum size of a touch target (Material), and 44 pt at Apple. A finger is about 1 cm wide. 24 dp icons need padding around them; Flutter does that for you in IconButton.
  • 8 dp of space between neighbouring targets. Two buttons stuck together are one button with a 50% error rate.
  • Thumb zone: with one hand, the bottom third of the screen is easy, the top is hard. Frequent actions at the bottom (navigation bar, FAB); rare or dangerous actions at the top.
  • 4.5:1 contrast for normal text (WCAG AA). Light grey on white in the sun cannot be read. We look at tools in a later lesson.
  • Text: body at 14 to 16 sp, short lines, and never disable the system font size.

For the tests: minimum target of 48 dp, minimum contrast of 4.5:1, frequent actions within reach of the thumb.

Navigation patterns

  • Bottom navigation: 3 to 5 top-level sections, always visible, each with its own stack of screens. Instagram, YouTube, our Tempo Açores (Forecast, Warnings, Settings).
  • Drawer: side menu for many sections or rarely used sections. Gmail. It is falling out of use because it hides the options.
  • Tabs: views of the same content, at the top, swipeable. WhatsApp on Android (Chats, Status, Calls).
  • List → detail: the most common pattern in any app: a list, you tap, the detail comes in, you go back. On a tablet, the two side by side.
  • Modal: a flow that interrupts (login, create a new item, filters) and returns to the starting point when closed.

On Wednesday, 7 October, we implement the first three with go_router. Today, only layout.

Empty, loading and error states are part of the design

  • Every screen that shows data has four states: loading, with data, empty and error. If you only design the second, the other three are blank screens.
  • Loading: an indicator, or better, a skeleton in the shape of the content. Never a white screen for more than 300 ms.
  • Empty: one sentence and one action. "You have no favourites yet. Tap the star on a location." It is not a bug; it is an opportunity.
  • Error: what happened in human language and a button to try again. "No connection. The data is from 12 minutes ago."
  • Apps that do this well: Airbnb, Revolut. Notice it the next time you lose network.

1. Constraints go down, sizes go up, parent sets position

// 1. The parent tells the child its limits (minimum and maximum width and height)
// 2. The child chooses its size within those limits
// 3. The parent positions the child

Scaffold(
  body: Container(color: Colors.teal),          // fills the whole screen: the parent forces it
)

Scaffold(
  body: Center(
    child: Container(color: Colors.teal, width: 100, height: 100),   // Center lets it choose
  ),
)
Ask the class If I swap Center for Align(alignment: Alignment.topRight), where does the square end up? And if I remove the width and the height from the Container that is inside the Center?
  • A widget does not know where it is and cannot choose its position. It only knows the limits it received.
  • The width: 100 on the first Container would be ignored: the Scaffold gives tight limits the size of the screen.
  • Flutter's most famous error, "Vertical viewport was given unbounded height", is a ListView inside a Column: the column gives infinite height and the list does not know what size to be. The cure is Expanded.

For the tests: constraints go down, sizes go up, the parent sets the position. A child never chooses where it goes.

Analogy The parent tells the child "you can be between 0 and 300 wide": those are the constraints going down. The child answers "I will take 200": that is the size going up. The parent then decides where to put it. A child never chooses its position and can never be larger than the parent allows.
Answers to the questions. With Align(alignment: Alignment.topRight) the 100 × 100 square sits against the top right corner: the size is the same, only where the parent puts it changes. With no width or height, the Container has no child and no requested size, so it becomes as large as the limits allow: Center gives it from 0 up to the whole screen and it fills the whole screen. Make both changes live and show the limits in the Layout Explorer.

2. Row, Column, Expanded and Flexible

Column(
  crossAxisAlignment: CrossAxisAlignment.start,   // aligns to the left
  children: [
    Text('Ponta Delgada', style: textTheme.headlineSmall),
    const Row(
      mainAxisAlignment: MainAxisAlignment.spaceBetween,   // one at each end
      children: [Text('Hoje'), Text('21 °C')],
    ),
    const SizedBox(height: 12),
    Row(
      children: [
        Expanded(flex: 2, child: Container(height: 40, color: Colors.teal)),    // 2 parts
        const SizedBox(width: 8),
        Expanded(child: Container(height: 40, color: Colors.orange)),           // 1 part
        const SizedBox(width: 8),
        Flexible(child: Container(height: 40, width: 300, color: Colors.grey)), // up to 1 part
      ],
    ),
  ],
)
Ask the class What changes if I swap spaceBetween for spaceEvenly? If the grey Container had Expanded instead of Flexible, how wide would it be? How would I make the teal one take three parts and the orange one take one?
Answers to the questions. spaceBetween pushes the first and the last to the ends and puts all the space in the middle; spaceEvenly shares the space in equal parts before, between and after, so "Hoje" and "21 °C" move away from the edges. Grey with Expanded: it would have the same width, one part in four of what is left (2 + 1 + 1), because the 300 requested is already more than that part and Flexible was already limiting it to that. The difference only shows with a child smaller than the part: with width: 40, Flexible leaves it at 40 and Expanded stretches it. Three parts for the teal one: flex: 3; the orange one keeps the default flex of 1.

2. Row and Column: what matters

  • mainAxis is the direction of the widget (horizontal in a Row, vertical in a Column); crossAxis is the other one. MainAxisAlignment distributes space; CrossAxisAlignment aligns.
  • Expanded forces the child to take the remaining space, divided according to flex. Flexible lets the child be smaller if it wants. Spacer is an empty Expanded.
  • A Row does not scroll and does not wrap. If the children do not fit, you see the yellow and black overflow stripes. Use Expanded, Wrap or a horizontal ListView.
  • mainAxisSize: MainAxisSize.min makes the column shrink to its content instead of taking the whole height.

3. Stack and Positioned: image with text on top

SizedBox(
  height: 200,
  child: Stack(
    fit: StackFit.expand,                          // children with no position fill everything
    children: [
      Image.asset('assets/img/sao_miguel.jpg', fit: BoxFit.cover),
      const DecoratedBox(
        decoration: BoxDecoration(
          gradient: LinearGradient(
            begin: Alignment.topCenter,
            end: Alignment.bottomCenter,
            colors: [Colors.transparent, Colors.black54],   // darkens so the text is readable
          ),
        ),
      ),
      Positioned(
        left: 16,
        bottom: 16,
        child: Text('São Miguel', style: textTheme.headlineMedium?.copyWith(color: Colors.white)),
      ),
    ],
  ),
)
Ask the class If I remove fit: StackFit.expand, what happens to the image? How would I put the text at the top right? And if I swapped the order of the image and the gradient in the list?
  • Stack overlays its children in list order: the last one ends up on top. Positioned anchors a child to one or more edges.
  • A gradient on top of the image is a common technique in travel apps to guarantee contrast for the text.
Answers to the questions. Without StackFit.expand the children with no Positioned are no longer forced to fill the Stack: the image takes whatever size fits while keeping its aspect ratio, pinned to the top left, and an empty strip may be left beside it; the gradient, which has no child, shrinks to zero and disappears. Text at the top right: Positioned(top: 16, right: 16). With the order swapped, the gradient ends up under the image and can no longer be seen: the last one in the list is the one on top.
