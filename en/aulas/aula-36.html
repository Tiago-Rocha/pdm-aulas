Presentations and defences (I)

First day of defences of the Tempo Açores project. Each group has 10 minutes of presentation with a demonstration on the phone, 5 minutes of questions addressed to each member and 2 minutes of feedback. The order is the one from the draw in the previous lesson.

Order: draw of 09/12
Arrive 15 minutes early: projector tested with your phone, scrcpy running on your laptop as a backup, USB-C and Lightning cables on the desk, timer projected on a second screen or on a phone facing the groups. Have at hand the assessment grid per group and the list of each member's PRs, with one question already marked per person.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Present your group's project in 10 minutes, with a demo on the phone and justified technical decisions.
  • Answer, individually, questions about the code you signed.
  • Listen to and use immediate feedback on the presentation and the product.
  • Assess, as a spectator, what works and what does not work in the other groups' defences.

Room rules

  • Everyone present the whole time. Nobody comes in or goes out during a defence; anyone who needs to leave does so in the swap between groups.
  • Silence, and phones on silent in the audience. The only phone with sound is the one on the projector.
  • Each group hands the teacher, before starting: the link to the repository with the tag v1.0.0, the link to the release in App Distribution and the backup video on a USB stick.
  • The demo is in release, on the phone, projected. If the projection fails, the group has 20 seconds to switch to the video. The clock does not stop.
  • The questions are addressed to one person by name. Only that person answers. A teammate can add to it afterwards, if the teacher allows.
  • The audience fills in the feedback grid from lesson 35 for each group and hands it in at the end. It counts towards the process criterion of whoever fills it in.
Read these rules once, aloud, at 16:00. Then do not go back to them: cutting at 10 minutes and addressing the questions by name are the two that are hardest to keep up, and they are the ones that matter most for fairness between groups.

What the rubric rewards today

Rubric criterionWhat the panel looks for in the defence
Functionality (35%)The minimums working in the demo: locations, forecast, warnings, synced favourites, offline. Extras shown, not just announced.
Quality and architecture (25%)Technical decisions with the why; knowing where each thing lives in the code; green CI on the tag.
User experience (15%)Loading, error and empty states shown without being asked; airplane mode with old data marked; a coherent app that is legible when projected.
Process (15%)Already assessed from the history; today it is confirmed: each member answers for their PRs.
Distribution and presentation (10%)Signed build distributed to testers; 10 minutes well used; smooth demo; questions answered clearly.

The individual mark adjusts the group's mark based on today's questions and on the PRs. Details in Assessment.

Typical questions: what each member should expect

  • About your PR: "Explain this function to me" or "why did you choose to do it this way?", with GitHub open on your code. One per member, guaranteed.
  • About resilience: what happens without network, with a 429, with a 500, with JSON that is not what was expected. The right answer points to the sealed class of states and to the retry.
  • About security: where the token is, what would happen if it were in shared_preferences, what is in the repository that should not be there.
  • About tests and CI: how many tests, what they test, what the CI runs on each PR, what is not tested and why.
  • About the test 2 material applied to the project: location permissions, WorkManager, deep links, versioning, what the Play Console would require in order to publish.
  • About what is missing: "what would you do with one more sprint?", with a concrete, ordered answer.

Answer briefly, concretely and with the file at hand. "I don't know, but I would look in X" once; making things up, never.

Immediate feedback: two minutes per group

  • From the teacher, 90 seconds: one thing that went well and that you should repeat in the next presentation you give in your life; one concrete thing that was missing or went badly, and what would have avoided it.
  • From the audience, 30 seconds: a classmate from another group reads the free sentence from their grid. No debate, no reply from the group.
  • The feedback is about the presentation and the product, not about the mark. The project marks come out after Wednesday, once all the defences are done.
  • Wednesday's groups hear everything today. They can, and should, adjust their presentation based on what they see. It is not unfair: it is what any team does when it sees the competition present first.
  • The audience's grids are handed to the teacher at the end and reach the group by email by Wednesday.
Write the feedback during the questions, not afterwards; the two minutes are for saying it, not for thinking. Record one line per member about the answer they gave: it is what adjusts the individual mark and what you will be missing when you close the marks on Thursday.
