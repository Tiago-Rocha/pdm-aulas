Crashlytics (Firebase, free), at Gameloft · Sentry, at Disney+ · OpenObserve, the one we are using, with errors next to the events and the recordings.

Open OpenObserve RUM in Error Tracking and show the Corvo error grouped: a single issue, with the number of people, occurrences and the crash-free sessions in the room. Open the issue and show the stack; the "Erros agrupados" table in the dashboard shows what the person did just before (depois_de). The island is Corvo: Corvo's forecast came with an empty list of days and the code reads the first day without checking. In the list it looked normal, because the list comes from somewhere else: the typical bug that only shows up with real data. The report gives the phone, the system, the browser, the time and the steps before; it does not say who the person is. In release Flutter is compiled, and without the symbol files the stack is a list of addresses: the symbols are uploaded at build time (--split-debug-info). The version is what decides whether you ship a hotfix today or it is already fixed. Disney+ has a published case on Sentry's site; Gameloft and Tapps Games appear in Firebase's case studies.

Two capture points in Flutter

FlutterError.onError = (details) {
  FlutterError.presentError(details);   // still in the console
  crash.record(details.exception, details.stack);
};
PlatformDispatcher.instance.onError = (error, stack) {
  crash.record(error, stack, fatal: true);
  return true;   // handled: the app does not die
};

The first catches errors in widgets (build, layout, paint), like Corvo's; the second, Futures without catch. For the tests: together they catch synchronous and asynchronous errors.

What to say: crash here is whichever service you choose; with Crashlytics it is FirebaseCrashlytics.instance.recordError, with Sentry it is Sentry.captureException, with PostHog it is the error method of posthog_flutter. sentry_flutter and firebase_crashlytics wire up both points for us. Without the first, an error in a build shows the red screen in debug and a grey screen in release, and nobody finds out. Without the second, an await that fails in a button is lost. In the lab Corvo was caught and showed an error state, like the states sprint 2 asks for: loading, error with "Try again" and empty. Quick question: in your app, what shows up if the forecast fails? Whoever cannot answer already knows what to test at the review.

Session recording

  • It is not video: it is the screen rebuilt from what changed, with text fields masked.
  • It shows where people get lost: the tap that does nothing, the screen that keeps loading.
  • Contra watched the recordings in PostHog, changed sign up and got 30% more accounts.
  • On the web there is Microsoft Clarity, free, and Hotjar; in Flutter, PostHog and Sentry.
Open OpenObserve RUM in Sessions and press play on a recording from the room, preferably one that went through Flores: you see the spinner, the slow warning and sometimes the person leaving before the forecast arrives. It is the slowness no crash log shows. It is legal if the privacy policy says so, if personal data is masked and, when the recording identifies someone, with consent (GDPR). In the lab the notice is at the bottom of the list. Banking and health apps mask whole screens. Contra is a case published by PostHog. Extra question: which screen of your app should never be recorded? Sign up and the profile.

  • Ship it switched off: the code ships in the release and is switched on later, first for 10%.
  • Forced update: "minimum version 1.4", and older ones show the store link.

For the tests: a flag switches a feature on or off in the version already installed; a fix through the store takes days and some people never update.

Project the terminal in pdm-tools and, next to it, the Laboratório tab of the dashboard. Kill switch: ask everyone to open Corvo, which crashes. Run ./lab_flag.sh corvo off. Within five seconds Corvo shows as "paused" in the list and whoever opens it sees "Forecast paused", with no error. In the dashboard, the "Kill switch: erros do Corvo por minuto" chart stops growing and the "Corvo em pausa" tile starts counting. What to say: nobody updated anything; the bug is still in the code, but nobody hits it until the fix reaches the store. A/B test: run ./lab_flag.sh ab on. Each phone had already drawn A or B; the B group now sees a bigger pink "Save this island" button right after today. Ask them to compare with the person next to them and save an island. The "Teste A/B" chart shows both variants, with how many opened an island and how many saved one. What to say: the group is drawn once per phone and always stays the same, otherwise people would see the button change; with one room the difference is noise, in an app with thousands of people this is how you decide which one stays. Offline, the app uses the default value. Tools: Firebase Remote Config, PostHog, LaunchDarkly. At the end, ./lab_flag.sh reset (with no arguments, ./lab_flag.sh shows the current state). In the lab the app asks every five seconds so it shows in class; a real app asks at launch and every now and then (Remote Config keeps the value 12 hours by default). In sprint 3, a group can put the warnings behind a flag and switch them on only when they are ready. Tapps Games has the case in Firebase's studies, with the staged rollout.

For the tests: crash-free users and ANR are the metrics the stores watch; above the limits, Google Play shows the app less.

What to say: these numbers are in Android vitals in the Play Console and in the Xcode Organizer, without installing anything. Above 1.09% crashes or 0.47% ANR, Google Play ranks the app lower in search and may show a warning on its listing. A useful alert is "crash-free dropped below 99% in the new version": it reaches the team's phone. An alert nobody reads is noise. Flores in the lab is the startup case: no error, only slowness, and people give up anyway.

Retro and sprint 3 planning

Retro (15 min)

  • Each person writes three post-its: start, stop, continue.
  • The group picks one of each and tells the class in one minute.
  • Example: "stop leaving PRs open for three days".

Sprint 3 (19/11 to 02/12)

  • In: what was left from sprint 2, warnings, reports, favourites and CI.
  • Out: new features without this done, big refactors.
  • Done: installable by someone outside the group, README with screenshots, CI green.

Sprint 3 backlog in issues by Friday. Sprint 3 review on 02/12; freeze on 09/12.

The typical mistake here is ambition: groups with sprint 2 halfway planning maps and notifications. Go round each group and ask "what happens if you only do half of this?". The answer must be "we still have the minimum". What was left from sprint 2 goes in first, before warnings, reports and favourites. Observability is an extra, not a requirement: a group ahead of schedule can wire up Sentry or PostHog in a PR.
