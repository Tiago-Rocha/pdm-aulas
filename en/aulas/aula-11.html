Accessibility and mini-project

Theory: what makes an app usable for people who do not see well, use large text or a screen reader. Practice: three accessibility fixes in Tempo Açores and, after that, the Trilhos mini-project.

Repository: pdm-aula-11
Two-hour lesson. Reference times: theory until 09:00; the three steps until 09:30; mini-project until the end. The mini-project is still due tomorrow, Wednesday, at 16:00. Accessibility does not count towards the mini-project grade, but applying it to the Trilhos app is a good extra.

By the end of this lesson you can

  • Explain why accessibility is mandatory and what ignoring it costs.
  • Make a screen survive the phone's large text.
  • Give the screen reader sentences that make sense: Semantics, semanticLabel and tooltip.
  • Check all of this in Chrome, without needing TalkBack.

Why

  • Law: the European Accessibility Act has been in force since June 2025. Shopping, banking, transport and service apps in the EU must be accessible.
  • Stores: Apple and Google review accessibility, and Apple shows "Accessibility Nutrition Labels" in the App Store.
  • People: about 1 in 6 people has some disability. And we all use our phones in the sun, one-handed, or with the system text enlarged.
  • Engineering: an accessible app has named widgets and a clear structure. It is also easier to test and maintain.
Ask the class Who has their phone text larger than normal? What about your parents or grandparents?
Answers to the questions. Usually several hands go up, and almost everyone has a relative with the text at maximum. That is the point: it is not a rare case, it is everyday life. In Android settings the text goes up to 200%; on iOS, with the accessibility sizes, even more.

Flutter does a lot on its own: Text, buttons and fields already have semantics. You handle what is missing.

Colour example from the mini-project: a trail's difficulty has a coloured dot and the text "Fácil". Colour alone would not do, because about 8% of men are colour-blind. Touch targets: IconButton and ListTile already have 48 dp; a GestureDetector around a 20 dp icon does not.

How to test

  • On the phone: TalkBack on Android (Settings, Accessibility) or VoiceOver on iPhone (triple-click the side button). With the reader on, tap once to hear and twice to activate.
  • Large text: in the phone settings, font size at maximum.
  • Today, in Chrome: the app has two switches in the Definições tab. Letra grande (200%) simulates the maximum text size; Mostrar semântica draws over each area what the screen reader reads.
Ask the class Five minutes with your eyes closed and TalkBack on: what do you think will go worst?
Answers to the questions. Usually: icons without a name ("button, button, button"), images that say the file name or nothing, and cards read in pieces ("Seg", "23 degrees") with no sense that they belong together. These are exactly today's three steps. If you have an Android at hand, demo it with TalkBack: it is the moment that sticks.

Step 1: large text

// with large text the chips grow, so the row grows with them
final scale = MediaQuery.textScalerOf(context).scale(16) / 16;
return SizedBox(
  height: 96 * scale,
  child: ListView.separated(
  • MediaQuery.textScalerOf(context) says how much the user enlarged the text. scale(16) / 16 gives the factor: 1 normally, 2 with text at 200%.
  • A fixed height around text (SizedBox(height: 96)) is the most common cause of cut-off text.
Do it Multiply by the factor the row height in DaysRow (forecast_screen.dart) and the width of DayChip (day_chip.dart, width: 72 * scale).

In the app: with large text on, the days get bigger and the yellow and black stripes are gone.

The cleaner alternative is not to fix heights at all: let the content decide. Here the row is horizontal and needs a height, so it scales. In the mini-project, the Wrap on the distance and duration row does the same job: if it does not fit, it moves to the next line.

Step 2: the screen reader reads sentences

// read as one sentence, not as three loose pieces
return Semantics(
  label:
      '${forecast.weekday}, ${forecast.type.description}, '
      'máxima ${forecast.tMax.round()} graus',
  child: ExcludeSemantics(
    child: SizedBox(
child: Image.asset(
  island.image,
  fit: BoxFit.cover,
  excludeFromSemantics: true, // the name below already says it
),
  • Semantics(label: ...) gives the reader one sentence; ExcludeSemantics silences the pieces inside, so nothing is read twice.
  • An image that carries information gets a semanticLabel; a decorative image, or one repeated by the text next to it, gets excludeFromSemantics: true.
Do it Wrap DayChip as above. In island_card.dart, the image is left out of the semantics. In island_screen.dart, the big photo gets semanticLabel: 'Fotografia da ilha ${island.name}'.

In the app: with Mostrar semântica, each day says "Seg, Parcialmente nublado, máxima 23 graus".

Typical mistake: forgetting ExcludeSemantics, so the reader reads the sentence and then the pieces again. Another: a semanticLabel equal to the name already written below it, so the reader says "Pico, Pico".

Step 3: icon-only buttons have a name

: IconButton(
    icon: const Icon(Icons.clear),
    tooltip: 'Limpar pesquisa', // read by the screen reader
    onPressed: () => setState(_search.clear),
  ),
  • The tooltip is read by the screen reader and shows on a long press. Without it, the reader only says "button".
  • The AppBar back arrow already has a name: Flutter handles it.
Do it Type "pi" in the search and add the tooltip to the ✕ button that appears.

In the app: a long press on the ✕ shows "Limpar pesquisa"; with Mostrar semântica, the button says the same.
